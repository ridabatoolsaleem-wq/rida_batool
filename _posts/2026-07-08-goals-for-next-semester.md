Every semester ends with results, but the more important thing it ends 
with is perspective. Now that this semester is behind me, I find myself 
thinking less about the marks I received and more about the habits I 
built — and the ones I failed to build. That gap between where I am and 
where I want to be is not discouraging. It is clarifying. It tells me 
exactly what to work on next.

The most honest lesson this semester taught me was about preparation. 
I spent too much of this semester reacting — rushing before quizzes, 
cramming before exams, scrambling before deadlines. It worked just enough 
to get through, but never well enough to feel genuinely prepared. Next 
semester, I want to change that pattern completely. I want to be someone 
who reviews material regularly, stays ahead of deadlines, and walks into 
every exam having already done the work — not hoping that the work I 
skipped will not show up on the paper.

I also want to take more ownership in group projects. My experience with 
the Pandas project was a clear example of what passive participation 
costs — not in marks, but in actual learning. When someone else does the 
work, you get the grade but not the knowledge. Next semester, I intend 
to be the person who installs the tools, prepares the data, writes the 
code, and understands every step of the process. That is the only version 
of group work that actually benefits me.

Alongside this, I want to improve my programming practice. Python remained 
difficult for me throughout this semester largely because I did not practice 
enough outside of class. Concepts that require repetition to stick cannot 
be learned by attending lectures alone. I plan to write code every day — 
small programs, simple exercises, whatever keeps my hands on the keyboard 
and my mind engaged with the logic.
