When the second semester began and I saw Database Systems on my timetable, 
I did not know what to expect. After a difficult first semester with Python, 
I was cautious about getting my hopes up. But from the very first class, 
something felt different. The concepts were clearer, the logic felt more 
natural, and for the first time since starting Computer Engineering, I felt 
like I was genuinely keeping up.

Database Systems focused mainly on MySQL, and compared to Python, it felt 
far more approachable. There was a structure to it that made sense to me — 
you create tables, define relationships, and retrieve data using queries. 
The logic was straightforward, and I found myself understanding things 
in class rather than going home confused and spending hours trying to 
figure them out later.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad taught this course as well, and his approach made a 
significant difference. From the very first lab session, he made sure 
every student was set up properly. He guided us through installing MySQL 
Workbench and WampServer step by step, making sure no one was left behind. 
That kind of attention to the basics — making sure the foundation is solid 
before moving forward — is something I had come to recognize as central 
to how he teaches.

One of the most engaging parts of this course was learning how to create 
websites using localhost. It made everything feel practical and real. We 
were not just writing queries in isolation — we were connecting databases 
to actual web interfaces, which gave the learning a sense of purpose. I 
could see how the things I was learning fit into something larger.
