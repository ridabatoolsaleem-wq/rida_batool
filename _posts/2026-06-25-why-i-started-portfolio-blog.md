When my professor suggested that I build a personal portfolio website, I did not fully understand why. I was a Computer Engineering student still trying to figure out how loops work and why my SQL queries kept throwing errors. A website felt like something for people who had already figured everything out — not for someone still in the middle of figuring things out. But the suggestion came from Dr. Bilal Ahmad, a professor at UET Lahore's Faisalabad Campus, and it made me stop and think more carefully.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad teaches us Programming Fundamentals and Database Systems, two of the most foundational courses in our degree. What sets him apart is not just the depth of what he teaches in class, but the way he thinks about technology in the real world. He is an expert in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning model training. More importantly, he is someone who believes that the work students do should not disappear into a folder on a laptop and never be seen again. When he told us to build portfolio websites, he framed it in a very specific way — he called it leaving your digital footprint.

That phrase stayed with me. A digital footprint. It means that somewhere on the internet, there is evidence that you existed as a student, that you learned things, struggled with things, and grew from them. In a world where employers Google your name before they even read your CV, having nothing online is almost as bad as having a bad reputation online. Dr. Bilal Ahmad pushed us to take control of that footprint early, while we are still students, so that by the time we graduate, there is already a trail of genuine work and reflection attached to our names.

The platform he recommended was GitHub Pages, and once I set it up, I began to understand why. GitHub is not just a place where developers store code — it is a professional network, a living portfolio, and a signal to anyone in the tech industry that you are serious about what you do. Having a hosted website on GitHub Pages means that every recruiter, every researcher, and every professor who looks you up can see your work directly. There are no logins, no barriers, just a clean URL with your name on it.

I am a student with very little background in computers before I joined this program. Programming did not come naturally to me. My first few weeks in Dr. Bilal Ahmad's Programming Fundamentals course were overwhelming — I did not understand why a missing semicolon could break an entire program, and I genuinely wondered if I had chosen the wrong field. But I kept going, and slowly things started to click. This blog is my attempt to document that process honestly. Not just the victories, but the confusion and the slow progress too.

The benefits of doing this, as I now understand them, are real and practical. First, it builds consistency. Writing about what I am learning forces me to actually understand it well enough to explain it. If I cannot explain something clearly in a blog post, that is a sign I do not understand it well enough yet. Second, it creates accountability. When you know that your experiences are public, you take them more seriously. Third, it builds a professional identity over time. Every post I add is one more data point that tells the world who I am as a student and as a future engineer.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad's approach to education is rooted in real-world relevance. In his research and ML projects, he often works with medical datasets — data that has been collected with high precision and accuracy, because in medicine, errors are not acceptable. He pushes his students to think the same way: to care about the quality of what they produce, not just whether it gets done. That mindset is exactly why this blog exists. It is not just an assignment I am completing. It is a practice I am building.

You can learn more about Dr. Bilal Ahmad's work and research through his LinkedIn profile at https://www.linkedin.com/in/drbilalphd/ and his Google Scholar page at https://scholar.google.com.au/citations?user=8nZ0jVkAAAAJ&hl=en where his published work in AI and ML is listed. For someone just starting out in Computer Engineering, seeing the kind of work that experienced researchers do is both humbling and motivating.

This is my first post. It will not be my best. But it is the beginning of something I intend to continue — one semester's worth of learning, one post at a time.
