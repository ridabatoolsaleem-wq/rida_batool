Before the final exams, two more quizzes were conducted. My performance in 
both was not good. I was still struggling with programming concepts and had 
not practiced enough to feel confident. Going into the finals, I was nervous 
and unsure of what to expect.

The final paper for Programming Fundamentals carried 60 marks. When I saw 
the questions, something unexpected happened — a portion of the paper 
included Machine Learning concepts, and I actually found that part 
interesting. More than interesting, I found it engaging in a way that earlier 
topics had not been. That genuine curiosity helped me focus, and I ended up 
performing better in that section than I had expected.

There was one distraction during the exam — my seat was near the door, and 
the movement outside broke my concentration a few times. But I managed to 
push through and complete the paper. Dr. Bilal Ahmad was the invigilator 
that day, and his presence ensured that the environment remained fair and 
disciplined throughout.

When results were announced, my overall performance was average. Not 
something to celebrate, but fair given how much I had and had not prepared. 
What saved me more than anything else was my attendance. Dr. Bilal Ahmad 
had always emphasized attending every class, and I had done exactly that. 
Full attendance meant I had heard every explanation, seen every example on 
the board, and absorbed more than I realized — even on days when I felt like 
nothing was making sense.

That is something I want to highlight because it is easy to underestimate. 
Showing up consistently, even when you are confused, keeps you connected to 
the material. You may not understand everything in the moment, but your brain 
is still processing. By the time the final exam came, some of that passive 
exposure had turned into actual understanding.
