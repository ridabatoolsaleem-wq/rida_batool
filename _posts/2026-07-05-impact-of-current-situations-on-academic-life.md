There are moments in a semester that no one plans for. This was one of them. 
Due to international tensions, Pakistan faced a petrol shortage that 
disrupted transportation across the country. As a result, our university 
shifted to online classes. It was an abrupt change, and like most students, 
I had to adjust quickly without much preparation.

Online classes followed the same timetable as physical ones, which helped 
maintain some structure. But the experience of learning through a screen 
is genuinely different from sitting in a classroom. In a physical class, 
you can ask a quick question, read the room, and stay engaged through the 
energy of the environment. Online, it is easier to zone out, miss something 
important, or simply feel disconnected from what is being taught. I noticed 
this in myself — understanding lectures took more effort, and staying 
focused required conscious discipline that I had not needed to practice 
as deliberately before.

Despite the difficulties, I kept attending and tried to keep up with the 
material. But during this period, something happened that I had not 
anticipated. Someone misused my username in an online class — joining 
or participating under my name in a way that was not me. It was a 
strange and uncomfortable situation to be in, especially because it 
involved my academic record and reputation.

I reported the incident to Dr. Bilal Ahmad immediately. What stood out 
was how he handled it. He listened, trusted what I told him, and dealt 
with the situation professionally and fairly. He did not dismiss the 
concern or make it more complicated than it needed to be. That response
